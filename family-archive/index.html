The user flow

One loop, from curiosity to contribution.

The information architecture follows the order in which people actually come to a family archive: first to look, then to listen, then to add, then to bring others in.

Information architecture

Five places, each with one job.

Features that used to compete for space now live inside the place where people would look for them. Timeline and Places belong to the family, so they sit in Family. Ask and Add are reachable from everywhere instead of taking a tab.

Always within reach: + Add to family (floating button), Ask about your family (Home, and from any person's page), and the mini player, which keeps a story playing while you browse.

Warm paper, dark ink, a little gold.

Atkinson Hyperlegible 17px base · controls and labels. Designed for low-vision readers.
Large by default

17px base text, 44px minimum tap targets, 56px primary buttons. A “Larger text” switch in Profile scales everything by 16%.

Words, not icons alone

Every navigation item and action has a text label. Nothing depends on knowing a gesture.

People are the interface

Faces and names are the main way to move around. Records, IDs and counts stay out of sight.

Cards only where they mean something

Most content sits on the page with rules and whitespace. Surfaces are reserved for the tree, players and privacy.

Gold means sound

Gold marks what can be listened to and what is selected, so older users learn one signal.

Private, visibly

The lock chip on Home, the role on every member and plain-language rules keep privacy in view.
